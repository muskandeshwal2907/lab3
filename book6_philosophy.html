Introduction: The Agora and the Examined Life (Excerpt)

"Under the Mediterranean sun in the 5th century BCE, a barefoot stonemason named Socrates stopped merchants in the Athenian Agora not to sell wares, but to dismantle the illusions of their certainty. 'What is justice?' he would ask. 'What is the nature of the good?' In questioning unexamined dogma, Western thought took its first breath. Two millennia later, Spinoza in Amsterdam ground optical lenses by day while demonstrating that God and Nature are one..."

Philosophical Scope & Treatise

The Golden Age of Philosophy is an epic intellectual chronicle documenting humanity's quest to decipher consciousness, ethics, metaphysics, and political justice. Spanning classical Athens, Alexandria, the Islamic Golden Age in Baghdad, and the European Enlightenment, Arthur Sterling illuminates how ideas ignite revolutions.

With luminous prose and profound scholarship, Sterling contextualizes Plato's Allegory of the Cave, Aristotle's Nichomachean Ethics, Ibn Sina's Floating Man thought experiment, Descartes' Meditations, and Spinoza's geometrical ethics.
